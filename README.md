# Minimizing_Carbon_Footprint_Using_Algal_Serpentine_Tubular_Photobioreactor
With rising CO₂ from steel &amp; power plants, this project builds a serpentine photobioreactor where microalgae grow by fixing CO₂ into biomass. Instead of waste, the biomass becomes pharma &amp; biofuel feed. A working model is testing CO₂ levels to maximize yield, making the idea green, useful &amp; future-ready.
The Industrial Revolution, rapid increases in air pollutants stem from fossil combustion,
 deforestation, mining, and vehicles.
 •CO₂, nitrogen oxides (NOₓ), sulfur oxides (SO₂), and methane (CH₄) have been released and caused
 historical threats, which include severe global warming and climate changes.
 •Besides the greenhouse effect, these pollutants also have health hazards such as asthma, lung
 disease, and eye irritation.
 •Carbon Capture and Storage (CCS) strategies now become breached point taking into their
 importance in dealing with any of these problems.
 •Microalgae-based photobioreactors, using species such as Scenedesmus, Chlorella, and Spirulina,
 provide a sustainable solution for CO₂ sequestration.
 •This initiative aims at designing an efficient photobioreactor in terms of algae production and
 optimizing pertinent factors, such as CO₂ infusion rate, light intensity, pH and temperature, during
 carbon capture
